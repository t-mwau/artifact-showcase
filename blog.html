Craft · Adornment

Beadwork carries more than colour

Across communities in Kenya, beads appear in personal adornment and ceremonial life. Their meanings, materials, and patterns are specific to makers and context—not one story for everyone.

Read the field note

Look closely at how beadwork is made, worn, and passed on. Ask who made a piece and what it means to them; the maker’s own account matters more than a broad label.

Craft · Weaving

Weaving shape from fibre

Kenyan basketry brings together skilled handwork and practical design. Materials, techniques, and styles vary, while contemporary makers continue to develop new forms and uses.

Read the field note

Notice the rhythm of a weave: the changing colour, tension, and shape reveal choices made by the person at work. Ask about the local name and material rather than assuming every basket shares one origin.

People · Dress

Clothing as an expression of identity

Dress can express belonging, personal style, occasion, and change. Kenya’s many communities and contemporary lives resist a single definition of what traditional clothing should look like.

Read the field note

When documenting dress, include the wearer’s voice and the setting. A garment may carry different meanings across generations, regions, and individual lives.

People · Portraits

Culture lives between generations

Knowledge moves through conversations, shared work, family, and community. Portraits offer a starting point—but the people pictured should shape how their own stories are told.

Read the field note

Respectful storytelling includes consent, names where people wish to share them, and room for people to describe their own relationship to culture and tradition.

Craft · Ceramics

Clay, shaped for daily life

Pottery and ceramics bring material knowledge into familiar objects. Today, artists and craftspeople bring together utility, experimentation, and visual language in many different ways.

Read the field note

Surface, shape, and firing all tell part of a process. Learning who made an object—and where and how they work—adds more than a generic description ever could.

People · Markets

Markets are places of exchange

A market can bring makers, materials, visitors, and ideas together. Behind each object is a chain of decisions and relationships that deserves to be seen.

Read the field note

Ask vendors about how an object was made and who receives credit for the work. Buying directly from a maker, where possible, can help support their practice.

Many voices. No single story.

Kenyan art and culture are diverse and continually changing. We aim to credit makers, respect context, and treat each story as an invitation to learn—not as a definition of an entire community.
